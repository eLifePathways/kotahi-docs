Kotahi is open source. You can download it, run it, and modify it, with **no
licence fees and no lock-in**.

Three things come with that, whether or not you ever pay eLife Pathways
anything:

- **Influence over the roadmap** — through the community, or directly through
  contributing code.
- **A community** of organisations solving similar problems with the same
  software.
- **Portability** — take the software and your content wherever you want,
  whenever you want.

What costs money is having someone else do the work. There are three ways to
arrange that.

## Self-hosted — you run it, we help

You run Kotahi on your own servers or cloud infrastructure.

- Editorial and publishing specialists help you set up and configure your
  workflow
- Technical support to get it running in your environment

Best for teams with their own infrastructure capability who want control over
where their data lives.

## Managed infrastructure — we run it, you drive it

You configure and run the editorial process. eLife Pathways handles everything
underneath it.

- Management and support of the publishing workflow, website and dissemination
- Hosted on low-cost shared infrastructure

Best for teams with editorial capacity but no wish to run servers.

## Fully managed service — we run all of it

A single fee for an end-to-end publishing solution.

- Publish in any model — PRC, preprints, Diamond OA and others
- Workflow co-designed with eLife Pathways' publishing specialists
- Submission, editorial and production services
- Peer review management
- Dissemination, plus maintenance and support of your website

Best for communities who want to publish without building a publishing
operation.

## Which one applies to you

Entitlements differ between customers, and the detail of what any given
organisation receives is set in their agreement rather than on this page. To
work out what fits, [get in touch](/about/contact/).

:::caution[Unresolved: service models vs. tiers]
The three models above are the public position inherited from the archived
Kotahi site. Internally, eLife Pathways operates a **four-tier entitlement
model**, which is a different axis — tiers describe what a customer is entitled
to, service models describe who does the work.

Publishing one while operating the other will make support conversations go
wrong: customers will arrive citing a service model that does not correspond to
their tier. **This needs resolving before launch** — either restate the public
offer so it maps onto the tiers, or keep tiers entirely internal and make sure
support staff know only the three models are public.
:::
