Kotahi is an open-source platform for **submission and peer review**. It is
built for the publishing models that do not fit traditional submission systems:
Publish-Review-Curate (PRC), preprint review, overlay journals, Diamond Open
Access, and community-run review of research objects that are not papers at all.

It is stewarded by **[eLife Pathways](https://elifepathways.org)**, part of
eLife.

## What you can do with it

**Run a submission and review workflow you designed yourself.** Submission
forms, reviewer forms and decision forms are all configurable without writing
code. You decide what data to collect, who sees it, and what happens next.

**Ingest preprints automatically.** Kotahi can pull metadata from preprint
servers, so a triage-and-review workflow does not have to start with manual
data entry.

**Publish where you need to.** Kotahi produces the formats and endpoints
publishing actually requires, including DOI registration and typeset PDFs, and
interoperates with the wider open infrastructure ecosystem.

**Manage the public-facing side too.** The CMS layer — now being developed as
**BATS CMS** — lets you run the website that presents your published content,
rather than bolting a separate system onto the review engine.

## What it costs

Kotahi is open source. There are **no licence fees**, and no lock-in: you can
take the software and your content elsewhere whenever you want.

What does cost money is *someone running it*. If you want eLife Pathways to
host, configure or support your instance, see
[Service and support](/about/service-and-support/).

## Why it exists

Most submission systems are built for one publishing model — a journal with
issues, an editor, and an accept-or-reject decision — and everything else is a
workaround. Communities experimenting with review and curation kept hitting the
same wall: the software would not bend, and the vendors had no reason to make it.

Kotahi's premise is that the workflow should be a configuration, not a
development project, and that the software running scholarly communication
should be inspectable and owned by the community that depends on it.

## Where Kotahi came from

Kotahi was originally built by [Coko](https://coko.foundation/) with a community
of publishers and researchers. eLife Pathways now stewards its development and
maintenance, and provides the services around it.
